For the newly arrived

Moved to Pune?
Your phone is not the plan.

You came for the job, the degree or the change. Six months in, you know three people and two of them are colleagues. This page is for you, and for anyone whose circle quietly got smaller.

Why it feels hard here

The city

It is big and it moves

Pune runs on pockets: a lane, a campus, an office park. Nobody hands you a group. You find a pocket and stay in it.

The habit

Plans live in chats

The good stuff gets passed around on WhatsApp and Instagram. If you are not in the loop, it looks like nothing is happening.

The fix

One regular thing

You do not need a bigger circle. You need one standing slot in the week where the same faces show up.

Pick a slot you will actually keep

Friends are a side effect. Choose the thing you would do alone anyway.

Before work

Early mornings

A run, a ride, a walk up a hill. People who show up at 6 AM are easy to talk to. They are all slightly out of their minds.

Weekend

One day out

A day trek or a fort. Four hours on a trail does what four months of text messages cannot.

After dark

A film, a gig, a workshop

Sit next to strangers who chose the same thing you did. You already have the first line.

The first visit is the hardest

Two lines get you through it.

Say this

"First time here."

It tells people to go easy on you. Most will.

Then ask

"How long have you been coming?"

Everyone likes answering it. You get the story and the next date.

Next week

Go back

Faces become names on the second visit. That is when it starts to feel like yours.

Everyone gets a seat

Pune has students, night-shift workers, people from every state and language, and people who are plain shy. Nobody here needs a pitch. If you are a woman looking for your people, we wrote a page for that too.
